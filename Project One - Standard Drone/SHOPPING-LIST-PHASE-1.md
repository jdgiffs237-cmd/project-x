## Three things that will bite you

**The smoke stopper caps at ~1 A.** It is for the *first power-up smoke test
only* — plug it in, confirm no short, confirm the FC boots. You **cannot spin
motors through it**; it will trip instantly. Motor spin-up happens on the bare
battery, after the smoke test passes.

**The stack is the clone minefield.** Search results are dominated by
"Compatible For Speedybee F405 V4/V5" listings. Genuine SpeedyBee boards are the
only ones the SpeedyBee app and its Bluetooth config will reliably talk to —
which is the specific reason this stack was chosen.

**The V5 stack runs closed-source ESC firmware.** The V4's ESC ran BLHeli_S; the
V5's OX32 ESC runs SpeedyBee's own proprietary firmware instead, which was
unpopular enough to be the main complaint in every V5 review. For Phase 1 this
costs you nothing — the FC still runs Betaflight, Bluetooth config still works,
and you actually *gain* ESC RPM telemetry. It matters later only if you wanted
to flash Bluejay or AM32 yourself. If that bothers you, hunt a leftover V4 at
ProgressiveRC rather than buying a clone.

**Why so much is gone at once.** New US tariffs on drones and drone components
(25–100%) were announced on 2026-08-14 and take effect **2026-09-03**, with
airframe parts and props hit at 25% from 2027-02-09. Retailers are being cleaned
out by pre-tariff buying and restocks will land at higher prices. SpeedyBee's
own store is already collecting a 54% prepaid tax on US orders.

**What this means for the build:** everything imported on this list gets more
expensive after **2026-09-03**. If the Phase 1 budget is going to happen at all,
it should happen before that date. The tools and consumables are the flexible
part — the frame, motors, stack, and battery are the ones to secure now, and
both battery options were in single-digit stock as of 2026-08-26.

**A note on the motors.** The 1855KV in the original list was never a derived
target — it is simply the winding iFlight sells the XING2 in. The actual
requirement, from
[03-component-research.md](../brainstorming/03-component-research.md), is a
*range*: **~1800–1950KV on 6S**, chosen because it is the most common 5"
configuration in existence and therefore the best-documented. The substituted
ECO II 2207 **1900KV** sits mid-range, and was already candidate #3 on that same
research table at ~$15. This is not a compromise forced by stock — it is a move
to a motor that was already evaluated and priced against the same criteria.

EMAX rates the ECO II 2207 at **3–6S**, 5–5.5" props, **1850 g max thrust on
6S** — above the ~1.5 kg the research table assumed for the XING2. Four of them
give ~7.4 kg. Against an estimated ~700 g all-up weight (not yet measured — frame
110 g + motors 126 g + stack ~30 g + battery 253 g + the Phase 2/3 gear) that is
roughly 10:1, against a documented minimum of 2× and 3–4× for sport flying.
There is enormous margin here; a KV difference of a few percent disappears into
it.

## Motor compatibility check — ECO II 2207 1900KV

Verified 2026-08-26 against the rest of this list. **Everything fits.** Confirmed
motor specs: 5 mm shaft, **M5** prop thread, **16×16 mm M3** bolt pattern, 31.5 g
without wire, 3–6S, 5–5.5" props.

| Against | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Frame** (Source One V6) | ✅ 16×16 M3 is the universal 5" arm pattern; V6 arms are drilled for it. **See screw-length warning below.** |
| **ESC** (SpeedyBee 55A ×4) | ✅ ~40 A estimated peak per motor against a 55 A per-channel rating. Comfortable. |
| **FC** (SpeedyBee F405 V5) | ✅ Motor-agnostic. Rotation is set in software, so you do **not** need CW/CCW motor pairs — buy 4 identical. |
| **Battery** (GNB 6S 1530 mAh 160C) | ✅ 245 A against ~160 A draw. **Resolved — the original Tattu 75C pack was the problem, not the motors.** |
| **Capacitor** (1000 µF **35 V**) | ✅ 6S peaks at 25.2 V. 35 V rating is correct; never fit a 25 V cap to a 6S build. |
| **Props** (Phase 2) | ✅ M5 thread, 5–5.5" — matches the 5" frame. |
| **Charger** (HOTA D6 Pro) | ✅ 1–6S. Unrelated to motor choice. |

**⚠ Screw length is the one real gotcha.** The V6 has **6 mm arms**, thicker than
the 4–5 mm most motors ship screws for. Two failure modes, in opposite
directions:

- **Too short** — only a thread or two of engagement, and a motor departs in
  flight.
- **Too long** — the screw bottoms out against the stator windings and shorts
  the motor. This kills motors and is a classic first-build mistake.

Target roughly **arm thickness + 5 mm** of thread. Check the depth of the
motor's blind holes against the screws in the bag before you commit, and do not
assume the M3×10 / M3×12 / M3×14 screws included with the frame are for the
motors — those are standoff hardware.

**Battery sizing — why the original pick was dropped.** Four ECO II 2207s at
full throttle pull roughly **160 A**. That figure is an estimate, not a
datasheet value — EMAX does not publish a max-current spec for this motor, so it
is derived from the published 1850 g thrust on 6S (~40 A/motor). Treat it as the
right order of magnitude rather than a precise number. Against that:

| Pack | Continuous | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| ~~Tattu 1300 mAh **75C**~~ | 97.5 A | ❌ ~40% short. Sags under punch-outs, runs hot, short pack life |
| CNHL 1300 mAh **130C** | 169 A | ⚠️ Adequate on paper, ~5% margin |
| **GNB 1530 mAh 160C** ← pick | **245 A** | ✅ Genuine headroom, +18% flight time |

**Treat published C ratings as marketing.** They are routinely optimistic, often
by a wide margin, so a pack rated 169 A against a 160 A draw is not actually the
comfortable choice it looks like — that 5% is inside the manufacturer's error
bar. The GNB's 245 A is the one that is genuinely not marginal. It costs $8 more
and 30 g (~4% of all-up weight), and buys back more than that in flight time
from the extra 230 mAh.

**Avoid LiHV for this build.** RDQ stocks 6S LiHV packs at similar prices and
they look attractive on paper, but they charge to 4.35 V/cell — a different
charger profile to get wrong, and 26.1 V sits at the very top of the stack's 6S
rating. Not worth the complexity on a first build. Stick to standard 22.2 V LiPo.

None of this affects Phase 1 — props off means the draw is a rounding error, and
any of the three packs would do. It starts to matter the moment props go on.

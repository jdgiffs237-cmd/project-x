# WI-02.5 — First charge, battery handling, and storage

**Needs:** charger, LiPo bag, multimeter. **Does not need:** the aircraft, the FC, or anything soldered.
**Never run this WI in the same session as soldering. A 480 °C iron and a charging 6S pack do not share a bench.**

Numbered 02.5 because it is independent of the build order — it unblocked the
moment the charger and bag arrived (Sep 11), and WI-03's smoke-stopper power-up
needs a pack with charge in it.

## Tools & parts

HOTA D6 Pro 325W AC/DC charger, GNB 6S 1530 mAh 160C LiPo (XT60), LiPo-safe bag,
multimeter, a **non-flammable surface** (tile, concrete, ammo can, baking sheet),
a timer.

## The numbers for this pack

Derived from the label — capacity sets charge current, cell count sets voltage.
The 160C/320C figures are **discharge** ratings and have nothing to do with charging.

| | Value | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Charge current, 1C | **1.5 A** | 1530 mAh ≈ 1.53 A |
| Power drawn at 1C | ~38 W | 25.2 V × 1.5 A (charger does 200 W on AC) |
| Full | **25.2 V** | 4.20 V × 6 |
| **Storage** | **22.8 V** | 3.80 V × 6 |
| Nominal | 22.2 V | 3.70 V × 6 |
| Land now | ~21.0 V resting | 3.50 V × 6 |
| Damaged below | 18.0 V | 3.00 V × 6 |
| Balance lead | 7-pin JST-XH | 6 cells + 1 common |

## Operations

### Op 10 — Build the charging station (10 min, once)

**Carpet is a fuel load. It is also the default surface in this apartment, which is
why this Op exists as a step and not a footnote.**

1. Pick a **non-flammable surface** and make it the permanent charging spot.
2. Charger sits on it with its vents clear — not on fabric, not on carpet.
3. Pack goes **inside the LiPo bag**, leads out the top, bag unzipped just enough
   to pass them. The bag contains a failure; it does not prevent one.
4. Clear the area: no loose wire, no breadboard, no jumper leads. A 160C pack
   will deliver 245 A into a short without complaint.
5. CHECK: nothing flammable within arm's reach of the pack.

### Op 20 — Inspect and measure before connecting (5 min)

**Never charge a pack that is puffed, dented, warm, or has been crashed.**

1. Look at and feel all six faces. CHECK: flat, firm, no swelling, no nicks in
   the wrap, no damage to the XT60 or balance connector.
2. Multimeter across the XT60. CHECK: **22.2–22.8 V** if it's been sitting at
   storage charge. Below 18.0 V → the pack is damaged; do not charge it.
3. CHECK: pack is at room temperature. A pack from a cold car or a recent flight
   waits until it isn't.

*(This is the same measurement as WI-01 Op 20.3 — doing either closes both.)*

### Op 30 — Configure the charger (5 min)

1. XT60 → channel 1 output. 7-pin balance lead → channel 1 balance port.
   **Both leads, every time.** The XT60 alone only shows the pack total.
2. Battery type: **LiPo**. **Not LiHV** — LiHV targets 4.35 V/cell and would
   overcharge this pack by 0.9 V across the pack.
3. Task: **Balance**. On this charger `Charge` and `Balance` are separate tasks —
   `Charge` stops on pack voltage alone, `Balance` charges *and* equalizes the
   cells. Always use **Balance**. See [Why balance](#why-balance-and-not-charge) below.
4. Cell count: auto-detected from the balance lead. **CHECK: reads 6S.**
   Reads 5S or anything else → reseat the balance plug. Do not proceed.
5. Current: **1.5 A**. The charger will do 15 A; there is no reason to.
6. CHECK the confirmation screen before starting: `LiPo · 6S · 4.20 V · Balance · 1.5 A`.

### Op 40 — Charge, and stay in the room (45–55 min)

**Never unattended. Never overnight. Set a timer for 60 minutes.**

1. Start. CHECK: current comes up to 1.5 A and mAh starts counting.
2. The animated bar at the bottom of the screen means *current is flowing*. It is
   not a progress bar. Judge progress by the cell voltages.
3. Watch the **cell spread** as it climbs. A new pack should stay within ~0.02 V.
4. Sanity-check the mAh counter against the expected fill:

   | Reading | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | Stops near 300 mAh | Terminated early — investigate |
   | **1000–1200 mAh** | **Normal from storage to full** |
   | Past ~1400 mAh and still going | **Stop it.** More than the pack holds |

5. Done when: **all six cells read 4.20 V**, pack reads 25.2 V, current has
   tapered toward zero, the charger beeps and the timer stops. Cell voltages are
   the authority — the pack total can read 25.2 V with cells badly uneven.
6. CHECK: pack is not warm to the touch. Warm after a 1C charge is abnormal.
7. Record the numbers (see [Pack log](#pack-log)).

### Op 50 — Put it back to storage (30–40 min)

**A pack left full degrades. This is the single biggest thing you control about
how long this battery lasts.**

1. Flying within ~2 days? Leave it full and skip this Op. Otherwise continue.
2. Task: **Storage**. The charger will discharge down to ~3.80 V/cell.
3. CHECK: finishes at **22.8 V**, cells even.
4. Pack lives in the bag, in the storage spot, between sessions.

**End state:** pack charged, cells verified even, returned to storage voltage,
and a baseline recorded to compare against in six months.

## Why balance and not charge

Six cells sit in series. The XT60 only touches the two ends of that chain, so
through it the charger sees **one number** and has no idea how it's divided.
The 7-pin balance lead taps every junction between cells — that's where the six
per-cell readings come from.

`Charge` pushes current in and stops when the **total** hits 25.2 V.
`Balance` does the same, but also bleeds current off any cell running ahead so
all six arrive at 4.20 V together.

Both of these are 25.2 V:

| | C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | C6 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Healthy | 4.20 | 4.20 | 4.20 | 4.20 | 4.20 | 4.20 | 25.2 V |
| Drifted | **4.28** | 4.25 | 4.20 | 4.18 | 4.15 | **4.14** | 25.2 V |

`Charge` terminates on both. In the second case C1 is overcharged — hot,
degrading, on the path to a puffed pack — while C6 is undercharged and gets
worked hardest in flight. The drift compounds: the abused cell loses capacity,
which makes it drift further next cycle. `Balance` resets that every time.

Cost of balancing: a few extra minutes at the tail. That's the whole downside.

## Pack log

Record every charge. A pack that takes noticeably less charge, or spreads wider
than it used to, is telling you it's wearing out before it tells you in flight.

| Date | Start V | Cell spread | mAh in | End state | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-20 | 22.80 V | **0.013 V** (3.793–3.806) | *(fill in)* | *(fill in)* | First charge. Cells factory-matched, excellent. Started on `Charge` at 0.9 A on carpet — restarted on `Balance` at 1.5 A on a proper surface. |

**Red flags:** spread over 0.10 V · a cell that sags far below the others under
load · a pack that gets warm on a 1C charge · any swelling at all.

## Disposal

A puffed, damaged, or crashed pack does not get charged again, ever. Discharge it
to zero (salt water bath, days, outdoors), then take it to a hobby shop or battery
recycler. Not the bin.

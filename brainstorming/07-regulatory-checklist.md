## The one question that drives everything else

**Are you flying recreationally, or not?**

"Recreational" means *purely* for fun. Not for a business, not for a client, not for content you monetize, not for your employer — even unpaid, even once. If any of that applies, you're under **Part 107**.

| | Recreational (Exception for Limited Recreational Operations, 49 USC §44809) | Part 107 |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot credential | **TRUST** — free online test, can't be failed, takes ~30 min | **Remote Pilot Certificate** — 60-question proctored exam, $175, must be 16+ |
| Registration | $5, covers **all** your drones, 3 years — required if drone ≥ 250 g | $5 **per aircraft**, 3 years — required regardless of weight |
| Under 250 g | No registration, no Remote ID | Registration and Remote ID still required |
| Must follow | A community-based organization's safety guidelines | Part 107 operating rules |
| Controlled airspace | LAANC / FAA authorization required | LAANC / FAA authorization required |
| Recurrent training | Renew TRUST as required | Free online recurrent training every 24 months |

> **Most common first-timer mistake:** assuming "I'm not getting paid" means recreational. Posting footage on a monetized channel, or shooting a friend's property "as a favor" for their business, is not recreational.

## Checklist

### Before first flight

- [ ] Decide recreational vs. Part 107 (above) → log as a decision in [06](06-decision-log.md)
- [ ] **If recreational:** complete TRUST, save the certificate (there is no central copy — if you lose it, you retake it)
- [ ] **If Part 107:** study, get an FAA Tracking Number (FTN), schedule and pass the Unmanned Aircraft General exam
- [ ] Weigh the finished aircraft **with battery and everything installed**. Record: ______ g
- [ ] If ≥ 250 g (or any weight under Part 107): register at [FAADroneZone](https://faadronezone-access.faa.gov/)
- [ ] Mark the registration number on the **exterior** of the aircraft, legibly
- [ ] Carry proof of registration (and TRUST cert, if recreational) whenever flying
- [ ] Sort out Remote ID (below)
- [ ] Identify a legal flying location; check the B4UFLY app / FAA UAS Facility Maps
- [ ] Get LAANC authorization if in controlled airspace
- [ ] Read the operating rules for your category end to end, once

### Remote ID

Any drone that **requires registration** must broadcast Remote ID. A custom build will not have this built in, so you almost certainly need a **broadcast module** (typically $50–100, a few grams). Budget for it in [04](04-budget.md).

- [ ] Determine whether Remote ID applies to this build (it does if the drone must be registered)
- [ ] Buy a broadcast module, **or** plan to fly only within a FRIA (Federally-Recognized Identification Area — generally at established club fields)
- [ ] Register the module's serial number in FAADroneZone (Part 107 registers each device individually)
- [ ] Verify it actually broadcasts before relying on it

> **Sub-250 g note:** flying recreationally under 250 g exempts you from registration *and* therefore from Remote ID. This is a real design lever — it's why so many builds target that number. But "under 250 g" means *ready to fly, battery installed*, and it is easy to blow past it.

### Operating rules that apply either way

- [ ] Keep the drone within **visual line of sight** (FPV requires a visual observer beside you)
- [ ] Stay at or below **400 ft AGL**
- [ ] Never fly over people or moving vehicles (Part 107 has specific categories; recreational guidance says don't)
- [ ] Daylight or civil twilight with anti-collision lighting
- [ ] Yield right of way to crewed aircraft, always
- [ ] Never fly near emergency response, wildfires, or stadiums with active TFRs
- [ ] Check for **TFRs** (temporary flight restrictions) before each flight
- [ ] No flying under the influence
- [ ] Check state, local, and property rules — the FAA owns the airspace, but landowners and municipalities regulate takeoff and landing

### Also verify (things that change)

- [ ] Current status of **BVLOS / Part 108** rulemaking, if beyond-visual-line-of-sight ever matters to you
- [ ] Whether any state or local ordinance applies at your intended site
- [ ] Whether the site is a National Park (drone takeoff/landing is prohibited), wilderness area, or otherwise restricted

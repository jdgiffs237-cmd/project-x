# 04 — Budget

**Last updated:** 2026-08-16
**Total ceiling:** **$10,000** (general working budget) — the number above which this project stops being fun.

> **Context for this number:** $10,000 is generous for this hobby, and it changes the nature of the planning. A complete first build costs $250–600; the full staged path through to a VTOL quadplane costs roughly **$4,600 including three aircraft and all ground equipment**. See [ideas/delta-vtol-quadplane.md](ideas/delta-vtol-quadplane.md) for that breakdown.
>
> The practical implication: **money is not your limiting factor — skill sequencing is.** So spend the budget on de-risking rather than on capability. Specifically:
> - Buy the *good* version of anything reusable (radio, charger, tools). These outlive every aircraft.
> - Fund the intermediate aircraft rather than skipping to the ambitious one.
> - Hold a real crash reserve. An expensive aircraft you're afraid to fly is the worst outcome.
>
> A generous budget's main failure mode is buying an aircraft well beyond your skill level and then losing it. Guard against that, not against overspending.

---

## Reality check before you fill this in

Three things blow up first-build budgets:

1. **Tools and consumables are a second budget.** Iron, charger, drivers, meter, spares — this is commonly $150–300 and is invisible until you're checking out.
2. **The radio transmitter and FPV goggles are not part of the drone.** They're often 30–50% of total spend and are one-time purchases that carry across every future build.
3. **Crash spares are not optional.** Assume you will break props immediately and probably an arm or a motor within the first few flights.

A useful framing: **aircraft + ground equipment + tools + spares**, budgeted separately.

## Suggested allocation of the $10,000

A starting split, not a commitment. Revise once the mission in [01-mission-and-use-case.md](01-mission-and-use-case.md) is settled.

*Revised 2026-08-16. Altius experience is **production, not engineering** — it transfers build skill, not design, tuning, or flying skill. All learning stages retained.*

| Allocation | Amount | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 0** — whoop + simulator | **$170** | Stick time. Currently zero, and unrelated to Altius. |
| **Stage 1** — **ArduPilot quad + GPS** | **$700** | Largest gap: hover tuning + the full autopilot stack. Avionics transfer to the quadplane. |
| **Stage 2** — foam fixed-wing | **$400** | CG on an unfamiliar airframe, stall behaviour, landing without hover. Cheapest insurance in the plan. |
| **Stage 3** — delta VTOL quadplane | **$2,500** | The project goal → [ideas/delta-vtol-quadplane.md](ideas/delta-vtol-quadplane.md) |
| **Ground equipment** | **$1,000** | Radio, goggles, charger. Reusable forever — buy well. |
| **Tools & consumables** | **$300** | Iron, drivers, meter, smoke stopper. *(Production background may mean some are already owned.)* |
| **Crash reserve** | **$2,000** | Ring-fenced for the quadplane phase specifically. |
| **Unallocated** | **$2,930** | Deliberate slack. Do not pre-spend it. |

> **Why hold so much back:** the most common way a generous budget gets wasted is buying capability ahead of skill. Leaving a third unallocated means you can respond to what you actually learn — including discovering the mission needs something entirely different.

## Guiding questions

- What's my *walk-away* number — the point at which I'd rather have bought a ready-made drone?
- Which purchases are reusable across future builds (transmitter, goggles, tools, charger) versus consumed by this one? Reusable gear justifies spending more.
- Am I buying for the drone I want in two years or the one I'm building now?
- Can I order from fewer vendors to cut shipping?
- Have I budgeted for a *second* battery? Flying with one pack means 10 minutes of flying and an hour of waiting.

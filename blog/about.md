## The project

Design, build, and fly a custom drone from scratch, starting from no hands-on
experience with rotorcraft.

I work on fixed-wing unmanned aircraft professionally. That sounds like a head
start, and in some ways it is — I'm comfortable with airspace rules, flight
envelopes, and why a spec sheet lies. But it's also misleading. I have never
built an aircraft with my own hands, and I have never flown anything that
hovers. Those are the two things this project is actually about.

## What gets written here

Entries come from a working repository — a brainstorming space, a decision log,
and an append-only build log. The blog is the readable surface of that; the
repo is the substrate.

Things I intend to write about:

- **Decisions and their reasoning.** Not "I picked X" but what forced the
  choice, what the alternatives cost, and what it now rules out.
- **Failures, in more detail than successes.** They're worth more later.
- **Numbers.** Weights, currents, costs, minutes of flight time. Stated in
  units, every time.

## The rules I'm writing under

1. **Entries aren't edited after the fact.** If I'm wrong, I append a
   correction rather than quietly fixing the original. A build log that has been
   tidied up is worthless for debugging.
2. **Write the same day.** A vague entry beats no entry.
3. **Record the reversals.** The decisions I back out of are more instructive
   than the ones that worked first time.
4. **State units explicitly.** Mixing grams and ounces is the most common way to
   order the wrong part.

## Where it stands

Design stage. No hardware purchased, no airframe locked. The survey work is
done; the mission definition is not.
